Interview Transcript — Aditya Rao — Backend Software Engineer role
Interviewer: Priya (Engineering Manager)

Priya: Thanks for joining, Aditya. Let's start with your recent work on the
payments platform. Can you walk me through the microservices redesign you
mentioned on your resume?

Aditya: Sure. I worked on breaking apart a monolithic payments module into
smaller services. The main challenge was the trade-off between consistency
and latency, since payments need strong consistency guarantees but our
users wanted faster checkout. I ended up designing an event-driven
architecture using a message queue so writes could be acknowledged quickly
while reconciliation happened asynchronously. That change reduced our
average transaction latency by around 38%, which matched what I put on my
resume.

Priya: Nice, that's a solid number. Did you personally manage the engineers
on that project?

Aditya: That's a good question. Honestly, I didn't formally manage anyone -
I don't have direct reports. I was the most senior engineer on the
reconciliation project and I coordinated the work of three other engineers,
reviewed their pull requests, and set the technical direction, but I wasn't
their manager on paper. I think I phrased that as "managed a team of 4" on
my resume, which in hindsight might overstate the formal management aspect
- it was really technical leadership, not people management.

Priya: That's a fair clarification, thanks for being upfront about it. How
about the Kubernetes migration - what specifically did you do there?

Aditya: I led the migration of two of our legacy monolith modules into
Kubernetes-based microservices. I set up the deployment pipelines, wrote
the Helm charts, and worked with the infra team on autoscaling policies. We
went from deploying once a week to daily deploys. I did run into a tricky
issue with connection pooling under Kubernetes' pod autoscaling - our
database connections kept getting exhausted during scale-up events. I
struggled with that for about two weeks before realizing we needed a
connection pooler like PgBouncer in front of Postgres.

Priya: Good catch. Overall, how many years would you say you've been doing
serious backend engineering work?

Aditya: I'd say around 3 years of real hands-on backend architecture work,
though my total professional experience since college is about 5 years -
the first couple of years I was doing a mix of QA support and junior
front-end tasks before I moved fully into backend.

Priya: Understood, that's helpful context. Tell me about a time you
disagreed with a teammate.

Aditya: During the ledger reconciliation project, one of the engineers
wanted to use a distributed lock for consistency, but I was worried about
the performance and single-point-of-failure risk. We went back and forth
for a day, and eventually we compromised - we used optimistic concurrency
control with a retry mechanism instead. I think that experience taught me
to slow down and actually listen to the trade-offs the other person is
worried about, even when I'm fairly confident in my own approach.

Priya: That's a great example. Last question - why do you want to move on
from your current role?

Aditya: I've grown a lot at Finlytics Pay, but I'm looking for a role with
more ownership over system design decisions from the ground up, rather than
mostly working within an existing architecture. I also want to keep growing
into a more senior technical leadership track, since I really enjoyed
mentoring the junior engineers on my team even without a formal management
title.

Priya: Great, thanks Aditya. We'll be in touch soon.
